Communication

Fifteen posts,
one feed

Facebook has no profile grid. Nothing is ever seen beside anything else, so the checkerboard has nothing to alternate against — the rule moves from the square to the scroll. What matters here is the order: no two posts of the same value in a row, and no two loud ones either.

The other difference is the crop. Facebook re-frames what we upload — the link card, the cover, the circular thumbnail — so the format is never entirely ours. We compose for what survives the crop, not for what leaves the studio.

Loud & quiet

The range is the point. A studio that says it reveals rather than covers has to be able to shout and to say nothing at all — with the same mark, the same typeface and the same black. On a feed that scrolls, the alternation happens in time instead of in a grid.

Maximal

The glyph is scaled past the frame and cropped. In 1.91:1 it is cut left and right rather than top and bottom — the wide card turns a letter into a horizon. One character per frame, never a sentence.

Minimal

Two dots, a hairline, a mark at its smallest published size. The empty field is the composition. In a feed this is what makes the next loud post land.

The alternation

Loud, quiet, loud — checked down the column before anything is scheduled. Two maximal posts in a row read as a shouting account, and the second one is the one nobody sees.

The formats

Five exports cover everything the page needs. The feed photo stays 4:5, as on Instagram — it takes the most height in the scroll. The 1.91:1 card is the one that is specific to Facebook: it is what a shared link becomes, and it is cropped and overlaid by the platform whatever we do.

Three rules

The scroll, not the grid

No checkerboard here — nothing sits next to anything. Alternate down the column instead: never two posts of the same value in a row, never two maximal ones. Read the queue as a sequence before scheduling it.

The card is cropped

A shared link becomes a 1.91:1 card with a title bar laid over the foot. Nothing important in the outer 10 %, and no display line that a crop could cut mid-word. Compose for what survives.

The circle

The profile picture is masked to a circle. The mark alone, never a lockup — the wordmark is illegible at that size. Reversed, paper on ink, so the avatar still reads against a light interface.
