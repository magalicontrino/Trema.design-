01
Manifesto
Reveal, never cover.

A tréma is two dots above a letter. A tiny mark that changes everything: it separates, it opens, it makes audible what was silent.

That is how we work. One precise, almost invisible gesture that reveals what was already there. A client's project does not become something else: it finally becomes itself.

Everything else follows from that: one shape, one typeface, no colour. This book does not tell you what to add — it tells you what not to cover.

Non-negotiable rule

The wordmark is a drawing,
never typeset text

The brand name is written trema, lower case, no accent, and it always comes from logotrema.svg. Never retype it in a system font, and never reset it in the display face either: the drawing of the letters and their spacing are part of the brand.

In running text, write "Tréma" like any other word, in the text face. The wordmark itself is never dropped into a sentence — it is placed.

Chapter 04

The mark
as a window

The strongest principle in the system: the mark is not laid on the image, it cuts into it. The photo fills the shape, the shape frames the photo. That is what makes a composition recognisable even once the word has gone.

01 · Photo cut out
02 · Solid
03 · Halftone
04 · Repeat

Cutting rules

The mark always keeps its 78 : 91 proportions. It may bleed off one edge of the format, one only. The photo inside is cropped, never distorted. One window mark per composition.

The subject — a screen, a page, a project detail — sits in the wide part of the shape, never in the point. The repeat tile measures exactly 78 × 91: no spacing, no alternating rotation.

32-bit PNG3000 px min.Transparent background

The photographs come from the places we work for — bars, cafés, kitchens, galleries. Colour stays in the picture; the system around it does not take any.

05
Typography
Reveal Never Cover

Three lines, one typeface, no colour. Each word is sized on its own so that every line bleeds by the same amount off both edges — the width of a word is its advance, not its letter count. The middle line drops to Light 300 at the same size: the loudest and the quietest setting in the system, one above the other.

07
Typography
Chapter 07

Two axes,
one signature

One typeface, two sliders. wght sets the weight from 100 to 900, opsz adjusts the drawing for the size it is set at. Neither of them is the signature. The signature is the tracking: display lines are set at −4%, so the letters touch and the line reads as one object rather than a row of words.

The signature — tracking 0 → −4%

Reveal the form−2% · headings
Reveal the form−4% · display

Why tracking and not a flare

The system used to lean on a flare axis that belonged to one licensed typeface. It does not any more: Inter is free, its licence is the SIL Open Font License, and it can be installed anywhere without asking anyone. What carries the brand now is not a rare letterform but a way of setting: tight, in capitals, sized so the line fills its measure exactly. That travels — it survives a change of typeface, which the flare did not.

Display sizes take −4%, headings −2%, and running text stays at 0 — below about 20 px, negative tracking closes the counters and the text stops being readable.

08
Typography
Chapter 08

Headings &
body text

Pairing rule: headings in Inter Black, body text in the same family at 400. Never set a long block at −4% — tight tracking tires the eye beyond three lines. One exception: the wordmark, which is not typography but a drawing.

Section heading

The heading carries the brand, the paragraph carries the information: it stays neutral, legible, with no character of its own. That contrast is what holds the page together.

Inter 900, tracking -4% — text 400

Second non-negotiable rule

Inter Black is set
in capitals, always

The flare is drawn on the capitals. In lower case the flared terminals and the descenders fight each other, and the line loses the flat top edge that makes the display sit square in a frame. So everything set in the display face — display, headings, posts, packaging — is uppercase.

Two exceptions, both of them deliberate. The character set in this chapter shows both cases, because it is an inventory rather than a setting. And the wordmark stays lower case: it is a drawing, not type, and the rule does not apply to it.

Letter-by-letter modulation

The one licensed departure from the scale: a line where every character carries its own weight. It is a setting, not an effect — the variation is written into the file, it is not triggered on hover and it never moves. So it reproduces identically in print.

Reveal the form

Where

One line per composition, never two. Opening headline, poster, post. Never on a paragraph, never in an interface.

How

The weight follows a function — ramp or swell — not a letter-by-letter decision. Two exports must give the same drawing.

Limits

wght 300 to 1000, slnt 0 to 15. The wordmark never modulates: it is a fixed drawing.

10
System
Chapter 10
trema studio draws , and for brands with something to show.

Pictograms are not illustrations: they are words. They are set inside the sentence, at the size of the text carrying them, and they stand in for a noun — never a verb, never an abstract idea. Three per sentence at most, or you are reading a rebus.

They are drawn on the same grid as the mark and follow the colour of the text: a pictogram never carries a value of its own. At display size, set them in Light — a long sentence in Extra becomes a wall.

Size

In a sentence: 0.84 em, aligned on the x-height. On its own: never below 24 px.

Colour

Always that of the text. Called as masks, never as images: they follow the polarity of the ground.

Rhythm

One pictogram per idea, three per sentence at most, always standing in for a noun. Set apart by a word, never touching.
