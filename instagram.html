Twenty-seven posts,
one grid

The grid has a job before it has a style: someone who lands on the account and scrolls three rows should come away knowing what a tréma is, what the studio makes, and who it makes it for. So the sequence is written to be read from the top — the sign, then the lesson, then the offer, then the work. Our clients are places you can look up on a map, and the mark already tapers to a point, so it drops onto a street grid as a pin without being redrawn.

Underneath that, the set runs on a single oscillation: a frame where the type is blown up until the format cuts it, then a frame with almost nothing in it. Loud, quiet, loud. Values alternate like a checkerboard — two posts of the same value never touch, side by side or one above the other — and twenty-seven, not twenty-six, gives nine complete rows with no orphan left hanging at the foot.

Loud & quiet

The range is the point. A studio that says it reveals rather than covers has to be able to shout and to say nothing at all — and to do both with the same mark, the same typeface and the same black.

Maximal

The glyph is scaled past the frame and cropped on three sides. You read the drawing, not the word. One character, one ampersand, one number — never a sentence at that size, and never a photograph underneath it.

Minimal

Two dots, a hairline, a small word in the bottom corner. The empty field is the composition, so nothing is added to make it look finished. If a caption would explain it, the post is not ready.

The alternation

Never two loud posts touching. A maximal frame is followed by a quiet one, in the row and in the column — the same discipline as the checkerboard, applied to volume instead of value.

The formats

We compose in 4:5. It is the ratio of the profile thumbnail and the one that takes up the most height in the feed — a square gets lost there, a 9:16 gets cropped. 1:1 is now only for partners who insist on it.

Three rules

The checkerboard

Two posts of the same value never touch. Across three columns: dark / light / dark, then light / dark / light. Check the grid before publishing, not after.

The rule of one

One graphic principle per post: the photo, or the pattern, or the display. A letter cropped by the frame counts as that one principle — it is not a licence to add a second. Never more than three typographic levels.

The wordmark

It comes from the file, never retyped. Set at the foot, centred or ranged left, never tight to the edge and never over the busy half of a photograph.
